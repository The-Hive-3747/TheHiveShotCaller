## DoD 2: "A 20-shot live practice session produces total shots, makes, make rate, per-ball-type breakdown, failure-order histogram, per-shot exit speed, launch angle and fitted equation"

### Reproduction (the exact CLI the lead asked for)
    python -m hiveshot.synth --out fx20 --bursts 4,4,4,4,4 --gap 3 --seed 4242 --fps 60     (default --spacing 0.25)
    python evidence/refute/run_fixture.py fx20 out.json        (Pipeline, realtime=False, until both files consumed)

Expected from shots.json (burst gap 1.5 s): 20 shots, 14 made (70 %), failure-order histogram {1:1, 2:2, 3:2, 4:1, 5:0}.

### Result: statistics are wrong on this fixture, and the failure is silent
| Run (code state) | shots recorded | made | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| fx20, lockstep, earlier code (~15:40) | **1 / 20** | 0 | log: `time offset cam B -> cam A: -0.2505 s`. Offset estimate locked onto one shot period; nearly all track pairs rejected. Hist {1:1}. Deterministic on rerun. |
| fx20, lockstep, **final recheck (current code)** | **17 / 20** | 12 (truth 14) | shots 17, 18, 19 (the last burst) never recorded. hist [1,1,2,1,0] vs expected [1,2,2,1,0]. `last_error` None, no status warning. |
| same, with 4 s of extra tail video | 17 / 20 | | same 3 lost, so not an end-of-file flush problem |
| fx20, realtime=True, run 1 (earlier code) | 20 / 20 | 13 (truth 14) | 3 make/miss errors (shot 0 made->miss, 17 miss->made, 18 made->miss), shot 16 unmatched + 1 spurious extra, burst indexes shifted for 17-18, 6 shots with exit speed error > 10 % (-75.9, -39.7, -20.8, -78.2, -44.1 ...) |
| fx20, realtime=True, run 2 (earlier code) | 17 / 20 | 11 | nondeterministic: differs from run 1 on identical input |

Root causes found by instrumenting the 2D tracks (evidence/refute/dbg_lost.py):
1. Time-offset lock-in. `Pipeline._choose_offset` tries candidates {0, estimate}, keeps the one with more matched pairs on the first recon cycle, and then holds it for 30 wall-clock seconds (`OFFSET_REESTIMATE_S`). With periodic bursts the correlation estimate is ambiguous (the recon module docstring admits it) and the first cycle has only 1-2 shots, so a wrong offset (-0.25 s at 60 fps, -0.30 s at 240 fps) can win and silently discard most of the session. It is timing dependent (race with the first recon cycle), hence the run-to-run differences.
2. Track identity swap in cam1 during the last burst: cam0 tracks are n=58, 46, 74, 55 (t 16.00-17.65) while cam1 gives n=57, 63, 45, 65 with different spans, so three shots find no partner and are dropped without any warning.
3. A lost shot is not counted at all, so it also shifts the burst index of later shots (realtime run 1: shots 17 and 18 got burst index 1 and 2 instead of 2 and 3), which corrupts the failure-order histogram.

### Where it works (not broken)
- Spec-friendly fixtures (spacing 0.3 s, first launch at 1.0 s, 60 fps, 3 seeds: ok20_777, ok20_4242, ok20_99, lockstep, current code): 20/20 shots each, makes 15/14/14 equal to truth, make rate exact, failure-order histogram exact ([1,1,2,1,0], [1,2,2,1,0], [1,2,2,1,0]); every shot has exit speed, launch angle, parabola a/b/c and drag fit.
- Realtime=True long session (20 shots over 37 s, gap 8, seed 777): 20/20, make/miss 20/20, histogram exact, speed error max 2.8 %.
- Real FastAPI server over HTTP (evidence/refute/app_fx20_s2.txt), fixture fx20_s2 (frame-0 start): 20 shots, equation/speed/angle on all 20, CSV has 20 rows, but made 14 vs truth 15 and one `unknown` (the shot launched at t=0, the first video frame). Make rate 0.70 vs 0.75; failure histogram {2,1,2,1} vs {1,1,2,1}. The "shot launched at frame 0" weakness appeared as miss/unknown in several fixtures on earlier code.
- Per-ball-type breakdown: the synth CLI cannot render NECTAR, so I wrote gen_mixed.py (8 shots alternating POLLEN and NECTAR colour/radius). Result exact: pollen 3 made 1 miss, nectar 3 made 1 miss, histogram {2:1, 3:1}. Small sample, one seed, not broken.
- Adversarial fixtures (earlier code, lockstep, 60 fps): overlapping balls 0.15 s apart (8 shots) 8/8 correct; burst of 6 then 3 (9 shots) 9/9 correct, index 5 and 6 bucketed into "5"; shots aimed at the lateral edge of the opening (5 in offset; 2 made, 4 miss) 6/6 correct; two bursts separated by exactly 1.5 s are treated as one burst of 6 (consistent with "more than 1.5 s"; note the measured launch times are frame-quantised, so that boundary is inherently a coin flip at 60 fps).
- Low arc (44-49 deg, the flattest the camera framing allowed; speeds up to 522 in/s): 6 truth-made shots, 2 classified `bounce_out` (shots 1 and 5, speeds 522 and 461 in/s). The pipeline extrapolates into the opening ("ball hidden") and misjudges fast shots; stats count them as failures (hist [0,1,1,0,0] vs expected all zero). Make/miss 4/6.
- `unknown` and `bounce_out` results are counted as failures in the histogram and make-rate denominator (design choice; it inflates failures in the 240 fps run).

### Weaknesses of the evidence regardless of verdict
- "Live" is not met: nothing ran on cameras. The only "live" evidence is a file-source server run (evidence/m4_app/live_run.txt).
- All footage comes from one renderer: flat grey background, uniform solid ball colour, no blur or noise, perfect calibration (the pipeline loads the truth calibration embedded in `*_truth.json` when no real calibration exists), and the truth "made" flag is computed by the same `field.segment_crosses_opening` that the classifier calls (circular).
- Only 2 committed fixtures, 6 shots each. No committed 20-shot fixture.

Judgment: **REFUTED.** A 20-shot session generated with the project's own tool at its default shot spacing returns 17 of 20 shots (1 of 20 on the earlier code; inconsistent in realtime) with no warning, so total, makes, rate and failure histogram are wrong. The feature works on friendly synthetic input only.

- Low arc, 60 fps: 4/6 = 67 %.

### Exit speed
Reference = `speed_ips` in shots.json (exact simulated launch speed; not a hand-timed measurement).
- 60 fps lockstep, spec-friendly (3 seeds): max error 2.8 %, median 0.01 %, none above 5 %. fx20 final recheck: max 5.3 %, one shot above 5 %, none above 10 %. (Earlier code showed ~0.00 %; current code has a ~-2.7 % bias on late-start fixtures. Inside 10 %.) Realtime long session: max 2.8 %, median 2.74 %.
- 60 fps realtime fx20 run 1: 6 of 20 shots above 10 % (up to 78 %): **fails the 10 % claim** (when the sync lock misfires).
- **240 fps (5 % claim), 20 shots, seed 777, current code: median 0.06 %, but shot 17 is off by -75.8 %; 19 of 20 within 5 %.** Seed 4242 at 240 fps: 4 shots above 5 % (9.0, 9.5, 6.0, ...) and one at -78 %. Cause: the first offset estimate was -0.2986 s (wrong) and the run only recovered because the 30 s wall-clock re-estimation (the run took 57 s) happened to fix it, and the new offset (-0.003 s) corrupted later shots. With the re-estimation disabled by monkeypatch (evidence/refute/ok240_777_noreest.txt) only 1 of 20 shots survives. On 8-shot 240 fps fixtures (17 s runs, no re-estimation): max error 0.04 % and 0.15 %, so the speed math itself is excellent when sync is right.
So "within 5 % at 240 fps" holds only when the sync lock happens to be right, and it failed on two of four 240 fps fixtures.

### Is "labeled test footage" real?
No. All footage, labels and "reference" are synthetic, produced by the project's own renderer, and the pipeline uses the truth calibration embedded in the fixture. There is no human labelling, no tape-measured or hand-timed high-speed reference, and no real ball footage at 30/60/240 fps (HANDOFF.md: "no real camera footage yet"). PLAN.md M3 explicitly asks for a tape-measured target, a hand-timed high-speed reference and human labels. My judgment: on the plain meaning of "labeled test footage ... against a reference", synthetic-only data does not satisfy the claim, independent of the numerical failures. If the owner treats synthetic as an accepted stand-in, the numerical failures above still stand.

Judgment: **REFUTED** (make/miss under 95 % on fresh fixtures; 240 fps speed and make/miss fail; no real footage). Detection alone holds on synthetic data.
